Ink-in-water wordmark

What this is: a fragrance-house landing whose hero wordmark is drawn into a live fluid field — a GPU flow simulation you stir with the cursor, smearing and chromatically splitting the letters like ink in water, then letting them heal.

Why it catches attention: type that responds to touch like a liquid is unexpected and tactile — you keep moving just to watch it bleed and recover. For a scent brand it stands in for the thing itself: something you disturb and that lingers.

Craft notes: two RG16F ping-pong buffers at 1/8 resolution — semi-Lagrangian self-advection, Gaussian pointer splats (radius grows with speed), dissipation 0.975/frame, magnitude clamp so hard stirs smear instead of wrapping; the composite displaces the wordmark texture and samples R/G/B at staggered offsets for a rose-and-gold chromatic fringe. Type is a high-contrast serif; oxblood rose is the one accent.

Reduced motion: the wordmark renders once, undistorted and still; no fluid simulation runs; marquee and reveals freeze.

The house

Scent is the
fastest route to
memory.

We compose in the dark register — resins, smoke, and flowers that only open at night.

Every bottle is a single accord held in tension: something bright that fades, something animalic that stays. Made in small batches, aged in oak, numbered by hand.

Nothing in the line is designed to please a room. It's designed to be noticed on one person, up close.
