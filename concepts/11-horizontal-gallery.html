11Horizontal Gallery

Pinned horizontal scroll gallery

A section pins itself to the viewport and converts your vertical scroll into horizontal travel through four exhibition rooms, each with its own internal parallax and a segmented progress indicator.

Why it catches attention: the axis flip is a violated expectation — the page suddenly moves the “wrong” way, which re-engages attention exactly when scrolling has become automatic. Because travel is still scroll-driven, users keep full control; it surprises without hijacking.

Craft notes: a 420vh wrapper + position: sticky viewport; track translateX maps wrapper progress with a 0.06 lerp so panels glide rather than track raw scroll; titles counter-translate 60px and glow orbs 140px for depth; progress dots fill per-panel. All motion is transform-only, on the compositor.

Reduced motion: the pin is removed entirely — the gallery becomes an ordinary horizontally scrollable strip with no scroll capture, no parallax.

An exhibition
of dangerous colors

Four pigments that poisoned, bankrupted, or obsessed the people who made them. The gallery runs sideways — keep scrolling.

Room 01 / Arsenic

Scheele's
Green

The Victorian obsession: wallpaper, dresses, candy — all laced with copper arsenite. Beautiful rooms that slowly poisoned their occupants. Napoleon's exile wallpaper tested positive.

CuHAsO₃ · c. 1775 · discontinued, emphatically

Room 02 / Empire

Tyrian
Purple

Twelve thousand murex snails for a single gram. Rome wrote laws about who could wear it; wearing it without rank was treason. The color was the empire's access-control system.

6,6′-dibromoindigo · c. 1200 BCE · 250,000 snails/oz

Room 03 / The dead

Mummy
Brown

Exactly what it sounds like: ground Egyptian mummies, prized by the Pre-Raphaelites for flesh tones. Production ended when painters learned the recipe — and when the supply ran out.

bitumen + myrrh + human remains · banned by decency

Room 04 / Radiance

Radium
Girl Glow

Undark paint made watch dials luminous and factory workers terminally ill. The women who painted them licked their brushes to a point. Their court case invented modern occupational law.

Ra-226 + ZnS · 1917–1938 · half-life 1,600 yrs
