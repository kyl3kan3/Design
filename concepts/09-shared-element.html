09Shared-Element Morph

Shared-element transition (FLIP)

Click any specimen: its artwork physically travels from the grid into the detail sheet and settles there — one object moving between two layouts, rather than a cut between two screens.

Why it catches attention: continuity. Jump cuts force the brain to re-find its place; a shared element carries the eye across the layout change, so attention never drops. The motion also explains the interface: this became that — model-building for free.

Craft notes: classic FLIP — First/Last rects measured, the element Inverted with a transform, then Played over 550ms on cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1). Non-uniform scale is applied to width/height factors so corner radii stay sane; detail copy staggers in 60ms apart only after the morph is underway (220ms delay); the return trip runs 420ms — exits faster than entrances.

Reduced motion: the sheet opens and closes with a plain instant swap — no flying element, no staggered copy.
