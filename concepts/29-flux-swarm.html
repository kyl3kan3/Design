29Flux — realtime data

Fluid-driven particle swarm

What this is: a realtime-data landing whose hero couples two GPU simulations — a fluid velocity field you stir with the cursor, and a swarm of 65,536 particles that read that field and ride its currents. Two systems, one feeding the other, both running on the GPU every frame.

Why it catches attention: thousands of points organising into flowing filaments — and reorganising the instant you move — reads as something alive and responsive. It's the product as a picture: streams of data finding their path in real time.

Craft notes: the fluid is an RG16F ping-pong field at 1/8 res (semi-Lagrangian advection + Gaussian cursor splats + dissipation); the particles live in an RGBA32F ping-pong texture (256²), each reading texture(fluid, pos) for its velocity plus a little ambient curl, respawning on death; rendered bufferless via gl_VertexID → texelFetch, additive, coloured by speed (violet slow → cyan-green fast). Float-buffer feature-checked with a static fallback.

Reduced motion: the simulation runs a few warm-up steps then holds a single composed still — no ongoing motion, no cursor stirring; marquee and reveals freeze.
